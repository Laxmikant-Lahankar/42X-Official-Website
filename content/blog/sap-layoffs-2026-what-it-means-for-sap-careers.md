## SAP layoffs 2026: what was reported and what SAP said

On 26 November 2026, Bloomberg reported that SAP is weighing at least 10,000 job cuts. The figure comes from unnamed sources and has not been confirmed. SAP says it is continuously reviewing its organisation, that no decisions have been made on further measures, and that it does not comment on speculation.

According to [CNBC's report of 27 November 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/27/sap-job-cuts-ai-restructuring.html), which cites Bloomberg, SAP employs more than 110,000 people. Every department was reportedly asked to identify cuts. Product, engineering and sales were said to face the closest scrutiny. [SiliconANGLE reported the same on 26 November](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/26/sap-reportedly-plans-to-lay-off-10000-employees-amid-ai-push/), and outlets note that the number could change, including through negotiations with worker representatives.

SAP's statement to CNBC is the fullest on record. SAP said "we are continuously reviewing how our organization and workforce need to evolve to remain successful" and "As part of this ongoing transformation, roles and functions will naturally change over time." It added that "no decisions have been made regarding any further measures" and "We do not comment on speculation."

Behind a number like this are people and their families. If you work at SAP, the most reliable information will come from SAP and your employee representatives, not from press reports or from this blog.

## Timeline: how we got here

The reported plan follows a 2024 restructuring, a 2025 shift to smaller yearly cuts, a July 2026 hiring restriction and a CFO comment on headcount in November 2026. Each item below is dated and linked to its source. Only the 2024 programme was formally announced by SAP as a restructuring.

- **23 January 2024.** SAP announced a "company-wide restructuring program" with "approximately 8,000 affected positions" and costs of "around €2 billion". It said most roles would be addressed through "voluntary leave programs and internal re-skilling measures", with a focus on growth areas, "in particular Business AI". Source: [SAP's press release, filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1000184/000110465924006500/tm244006d1_ex99-2.htm).
- **24 July 2024.** The estimate rose to 9,000–10,000 positions and total costs to "around €3 billion". SAP said the extra cost would "primarily reflect the positive response to the volunteer programs." Source: [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/3477211/sap-restructuring-to-impact-more-jobs-than-expected.html).
- **10 September 2025.** heise reported that SAP had moved to smaller, ongoing cuts. CEO Christian Klein told the dpa news agency: "In one year, it could be one or sometimes two percent of jobs that are eliminated." Source: [heise](https://heise.de/-10639836).
- **2 July 2026.** An internal memo restricted hiring to "selected profiles only, mainly core AI roles". Travel unrelated to AI was paused and supplier spending was to be cut. A spokesperson said customer-facing work and critical AI projects remained fully funded. Source: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/sap-hiring-travel-freeze-ai-push).
- **21 October 2026.** SAP's Q3 2026 results reported cloud revenue up 26% and total quarterly revenue above €10 billion for the first time. The release does not mention headcount or restructuring. Source: [SAP News](https://news.sap.com/?p=248420).
- **19 November 2026.** CFO Dominik Asam told a Morgan Stanley conference he "would be really surprised if our headcount would not, kind of go the other way going forward". Source: [CFO Dive, 23 November 2026](https://www.cfodive.com/news/ai-productivity-gains-slow-headcount-sap-cfo-predicts/875530/).
- **26–27 November 2026.** Bloomberg reported that SAP is weighing at least 10,000 job cuts. CNBC and others followed up on 27 November with SAP's response. Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/27/sap-job-cuts-ai-restructuring.html).

## What we still don't know

Almost everything that matters to affected people is still unknown. SAP has not said whether it will announce cuts, when, how many, in which countries, or whether they would be voluntary or compulsory. Until SAP publishes something, treat any precise claim on these points as speculation.

- **Timing.** SiliconANGLE noted Bloomberg's point that SAP has historically announced workforce reductions in January. [Brussels Signal](https://brusselssignal.eu/2026/11/sap-weighs-cutting-at-least-10000-jobs-in-ai-overhaul/) expects an announcement in early 2027, likely alongside the late-January annual results. That is an outlet's expectation, not SAP's.
- **Final number.** Reports describe 10,000 as a minimum that could change.
- **Countries.** No source we reviewed gives a breakdown by country. Reports also differ on what a German works-council agreement means, so we draw no conclusions about any country.
- **Form.** In 2024, SAP relied mostly on voluntary leave and reskilling. Whether any new measures would follow that approach is unknown.

## SAP's own workforce vs demand for SAP skills

Two different things share the word "SAP". The reported cuts concern SAP SE's own employees, who build, sell and support SAP's products. Demand for SAP skills comes from the thousands of companies that run SAP and the partners who implement it. Any link between the two is our analysis, not a reported fact.

None of the sources in our research say that cuts at SAP will mean more or fewer jobs for SAP consultants at customers or partners. The reports name product, engineering and sales inside SAP. The skills data below comes from surveys of SAP customers and from recruiters.

So keep two questions apart. "Is SAP cutting jobs?" is about one employer, and the answer is not yet confirmed. "Do companies still need people who can run and migrate SAP?" is about the wider market, and the sources below speak to it.

## What it means for SAP careers: deadlines and the skills gap

SAP's official timeline keeps migration work on the agenda. Mainstream maintenance for core ECC applications ends in 2027, with optional extended maintenance to 2030. Surveys in 2026 reported that many customers have not finished moving to SAP S/4HANA, that projects run late, and that some skills are scarce.

**The deadline is SAP's own.** In its [February 2020 announcement](https://news.sap.com/2020/02/sap-s4hana-maintenance-2040-clarity-choice-sap-business-suite-7/), SAP said mainstream maintenance for core SAP Business Suite 7 applications (the suite that includes ECC) runs to the end of 2027. Optional extended maintenance runs to the end of 2030, at a premium of two percentage points on the maintenance basis. SAP committed to maintaining S/4HANA through the end of 2040.

**Many customers are mid-journey.** [SAPinsider's 2026 benchmark](https://sapinsider.org/blogs/sap-s4hana-erp-migration-trends-2026/) (2 April 2026) found that 55% of respondents have deployed S/4HANA or S/4HANA Cloud, but "only 34% have fully transitioned". It estimates that 20,000–25,000 legacy SAP ERP customers are not yet licensed for S/4HANA, and expects this to create a "talent bottleneck". 39% pointed to the 2027 deadline as a factor in their ERP strategy.

**Projects are hard, and people are the constraint.** An ISG survey of 200 senior decision-makers, reported by [The Register on 5 February 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/05/sap_migrations_research/), found that "nearly 60%" of SAP migration projects are delayed and over budget. An ISG analyst said: "A lot of the delays are caused by people, not necessarily the technology." The same article cites Gartner data that 39% of SAP's 35,000 ECC customers had not migrated as of Q4 2024.

**Some skills are scarce, at least in Europe.** Recruiter Eursap told [IT Brief on 6 March 2026](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/eu-faces-growing-shortage-of-sap-s-4hana-specialists) that the hardest roles to fill are S/4HANA programme managers, solution architects and senior functional consultants. It named these skills:

| Area | What Eursap reported (IT Brief, March 2026) |
|---|---|
| Scarce modules | FI/CO, SD, MM and EWM |
| Skills employers want | Fiori, Business Technology Platform (BTP), embedded analytics, API integration |
| On ABAP | "classic ABAP knowledge alone is no longer enough" |
| Time to hire | Senior SAP roles often take well over 90 days to fill |

This is one recruiter's view of the European market, not a global survey.

## What this means if you are building an SAP career

Our reading, and it is an interpretation, is that the reported cuts are news about one employer's restructuring. They are not evidence that SAP skills have stopped mattering. The sourced picture is of customers facing fixed deadlines and projects short of people with current, hands-on skills.

A few practical points follow from the sources:

- **Learn the S/4HANA way of working.** The scarce skills named above are tied to S/4HANA projects, not older releases.
- **Go deep in one module, then learn how it connects.** Eursap's scarce list is made up of core business modules. Depth plus integration knowledge is what projects use.
- **Add the surrounding skills over time.** Fiori, BTP and API integration came up repeatedly. You do not need all of them on day one, but a plan to grow into them helps.
- **Be wary of anyone using news like this to rush you.** A good training decision is made calmly, based on your background and goals.
