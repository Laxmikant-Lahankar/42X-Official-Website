## First, functional vs technical SAP roles

SAP roles split into two families. Functional roles work on business processes and configure SAP to run them: MM for buying, SD for selling, EWM for warehousing. Technical roles work on the system itself: ABAP developers write and fix code, and BASIS administrators install, connect and run SAP systems. Decide which family suits you before picking a module.

**Functional consultants** spend their days with business users. They run workshops, understand how purchasing or sales really works in a company, configure SAP to match, test it and support it after go-live. Their main tools are process knowledge, configuration and clear communication. Coding is not the job, though reading a debugger helps.

**Technical consultants** spend more time inside the system. An ABAP developer builds reports, enhancements, interfaces and data migrations that standard SAP does not cover. A BASIS administrator manages users and roles, moves changes between systems, schedules jobs, monitors performance and keeps the landscape healthy.

Neither family is better. They suit different people. If you enjoy explaining processes and working with people, functional work is usually a better fit. If you enjoy logic, code or systems, technical work probably is.

## Which SAP module is best for freshers? Match it to your background

The most reliable way to choose is to start from what you already know. A commerce graduate already understands invoices and receivables. A mechanical engineer already understands materials and production. A programmer already thinks in logic. Use the matrix below to find your best fit, a sensible second option, and the signs that a module is wrong for you.

| Your degree or experience | Best fit | Also consider | Avoid if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| B.Com, BBA or MBA in finance | SAP FICO (not taught at 42X); among our tracks, SAP SD or SAP MM | MM if you like operations and invoices; SD if you like billing and customers | You picked a module only because a friend did |
| MBA in marketing or sales, or sales experience | SAP SD | SAP MM, since the two modules meet constantly | You dislike detailed pricing rules and document flows |
| Mechanical, production or industrial engineering | SAP MM | SAP EWM, or ABAP if you already code | You want to avoid business users entirely; then look at the technical tracks |
| Supply chain, logistics or warehouse experience | SAP EWM or SAP MM | The other of the two; MM plus EWM is a common pairing | You have no interest in physical operations |
| CS, IT, BCA or MCA | SAP ABAP | SAP BASIS, or a functional module if you prefer people over code | You do not enjoy programming; do not force ABAP |
| IT infrastructure, system admin or support | SAP BASIS | SAP ABAP if you also script or code | You want to work on business processes rather than systems |

Two notes on the matrix. First, it is a starting point, not a rule. Plenty of engineers become excellent SD consultants. Second, "avoid if" matters as much as "best fit". Choosing a module that goes against your interests is the most common reason people stall.

## The five modules in brief

Each module below is described the same way: what you work on, a typical problem, who it suits and how long the 42X track runs. All five tracks need no prior SAP experience, include 1-on-1 mentorship and a capstone, and run both live online and in a classroom. Plan for 8 to 12 hours a week.

### SAP MM: procure-to-pay

SAP MM (Materials Management) runs everything a company buys. You work with purchase requisitions, purchase orders, goods receipts, stock movements, supplier invoices and material requirements planning (MRP). Special cases such as subcontracting, consignment and stock transfers between plants are part of the job.

A typical problem: a supplier invoice is blocked for a price variance. You check the purchase order history and the goods receipt / invoice receipt (GR/IR) position, and find that a wrong price in the info record caused it.

MM suits people from purchasing, supply chain, stores or engineering who like structured, operational work. The 42X track runs about 15 weeks, with 13 modules plus a capstone that simulates a full procure-to-pay cycle. See the [SAP MM course](/courses/sap-mm) for details.

### SAP SD: order-to-cash

SAP SD (Sales and Distribution) runs everything a company sells. You work with quotations, sales orders, pricing, availability checks, credit management, deliveries, billing and returns. Pricing is a big part of the module, because every discount, surcharge and freight rule has to come out right on the order.

A typical problem: a sales order is stuck on credit block and the customer is waiting. You read the credit check and the pricing log, and find that a wrong condition record inflated the order value.

SD suits people from sales, customer service, commerce or marketing who like customer-facing processes. The 42X track runs about 13 weeks, with 12 modules plus an order-to-cash capstone. See the [SAP SD course](/courses/sap-sd). If you are torn between these two, read [SAP MM vs SAP SD](/blog/sap-mm-vs-sap-sd).

### SAP EWM: warehouse operations

SAP EWM (Extended Warehouse Management) controls what happens inside a warehouse: goods receipt, putaway, picking, packing, staging, goods issue and waves. Advanced topics include yard management, slotting, cross-docking, kitting and production supply. It is SAP's direction for warehousing in SAP S/4HANA.

A typical problem: a warehouse task is stuck in a queue and outbound shipping has stopped. You trace how the task was created and find a wrong storage type search sequence.

EWM suits logistics and warehouse people, and MM consultants who want to go deeper into execution. The 42X track runs about 14 weeks, with 11 modules plus a warehouse capstone. See the [SAP EWM course](/courses/sap-ewm). If you have heard of "WM" in job ads, our post on [SAP WM vs EWM](/blog/sap-wm-vs-ewm) explains the difference.

### SAP ABAP: development

ABAP is SAP's own programming language. ABAP developers build what standard SAP does not provide: custom reports, screens, enhancements and BAdIs (Business Add-Ins), interfaces using BAPIs and RFCs, data migrations, and the back end of Fiori apps through OData services.

A typical problem: a custom report times out in the test system. You run an SQL trace and find a database SELECT inside a loop, rewrite the query and get the code reviewed.

ABAP suits people who enjoy programming, including CS and IT graduates and developers from other languages. Any programming background helps, but the course starts from fundamentals. The 42X track runs about 13.5 weeks, with 9 modules plus a capstone. See the [SAP ABAP course](/courses/sap-abap), and read [how to become an SAP ABAP developer](/blog/how-to-become-sap-abap-developer) for the full roadmap.

### SAP BASIS: administration

SAP BASIS is the technical foundation that keeps SAP systems running. BASIS administrators manage users and roles, move changes through the development, quality and production landscape with transports, schedule and monitor background jobs, look after the database and handle patches and security.

A typical problem: short dumps start spiking in production. You check the dump analysis and system log, trace the cause to a failed chain of background jobs, reschedule them and file an incident report.

BASIS suits people from IT infrastructure, system administration or support who like keeping systems healthy. Comfort with operating systems and networking basics helps. The 42X track runs about 12.5 weeks, with 9 modules plus a capstone. See the [SAP BASIS course](/courses/sap-basis), and read [what is SAP BASIS](/blog/what-is-sap-basis) for a closer look at the role.

## What about FICO, SuccessFactors and others?

SAP has many more modules than the five above. SAP FICO (Financial Accounting and Controlling) is a popular choice for finance and accounting backgrounds. SAP SuccessFactors is a common path for HR backgrounds. 42X Academy does not currently teach either. If your background points clearly to one of them, that may be the right choice for you.

We say this plainly because a good decision matters more than a sale. A commerce graduate who loves accounting might be happiest in FICO. An HR professional might be best served by SuccessFactors. Other modules, such as Production Planning (PP) and Quality Management (QM), also connect closely to the logistics tracks.

That said, the five tracks we teach are not narrow choices. MM and SD both post into finance constantly, so a commerce graduate with an eye for operations or billing can do well in either. And knowing how your module connects to finance is useful whichever module you choose.

If you want to explore before committing, SAP offers free, self-paced learning on [SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/). The [SAP S/4HANA Sales overview](https://learning.sap.com/products/supply-chain-management/sales) is a good example of how SAP describes a module in its own words. Technical readers can try the free tutorials on the [SAP Developer Center](https://developers.sap.com/learn/).

## Mistakes to avoid when picking a module

Most bad module choices come from the same few mistakes: following hype or salary rumours, forcing yourself into ABAP without enjoying code, ignoring how modules connect, and trusting institutes that promise placement or official certification. Avoid these and your choice is already better than most.

**Choosing by hype or salary rumours.** Social media is full of claims that one module "pays the most". We have found no reliable, dated public source that supports such claims. Pay depends on company, project, experience and skill. A module you enjoy is one you will get good at, and that matters far more.

**Forcing ABAP when you do not enjoy code.** ABAP is a real programming job. If you dislike programming, you will find it hard to stay motivated, and functional work may suit you better.

**Ignoring integration.** No SAP module works alone. MM feeds stock to SD and EWM. SD billing and MM invoices post to finance. Whichever module you pick, learn how it connects to the others. That is often what separates a useful consultant from someone who only knows screens.

**Trusting "guaranteed placement" or "official certification included".** No training institute can guarantee you a job. An institute's course certificate is also not an SAP certification. SAP runs its own exams, which you book separately through SAP Learning.

## A 3-question self-test

Answer three quick questions honestly. Do you enjoy writing code? Do you prefer working on business processes or on systems? Which industry or function do you already know? Your answers point to a module family, and then to a specific module. Use this as a starting point for a conversation, not a final verdict.

**1. Do you enjoy writing code?**

- **Yes, and I would like to do it every day:** go to question 2b.
- **No, or only a little:** go to question 2a.

**2a. You prefer business processes. Which side do you understand best?**

- Buying, suppliers, stores or production planning: **SAP MM**.
- Selling, customers, pricing or billing: **SAP SD**.
- Physical warehouse work, logistics or distribution: **SAP EWM**.
- Accounting or HR: FICO or SuccessFactors may fit better (see above).

**2b. You prefer technical work. What draws you more?**

- Building things: programs, reports, interfaces: **SAP ABAP**.
- Running things: servers, users, performance, uptime: **SAP BASIS**.

**3. Which industry do you already know?**

Use this as a tie-breaker. Manufacturing experience strengthens MM. Retail and distribution strengthen SD or EWM. Logistics and e-commerce strengthen EWM. IT services strengthen ABAP or BASIS.

If two answers conflict, that is normal. Read the comparison posts for the modules you are torn between, or talk it through with someone who has worked on SAP projects.
